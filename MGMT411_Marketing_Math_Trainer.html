    Break-even
    Break-even volume is where profit = 0, i.e. total revenue = total cost.
    P × Q = (UVC × Q) + FC
    Unit contribution (margin per unit) = P − UVC
    BEV = FC ÷ (P − UVC)
    • P = price per unit
    • Q = units sold
    • UVC = variable cost per unit
    • FC = total fixed cost
    Adding a profit goal
    A target profit behaves exactly like more fixed cost — add it to the numerator.
    Q = (FC + target profit) ÷ (P − UVC)
    And running it the other direction:
    Profit = Q × (P − UVC) − FC
    Margin % and markup %
    Same numerator, different denominator.
    Margin % = (P − VC) ÷ P
    Markup % = (P − VC) ÷ VC
    If you are not the manufacturer, VC is the cost to buy from your supplier and P is your selling price.
    Converting between them
    Margin = Markup ÷ (1 + Markup)
    Markup = Margin ÷ (1 − Margin)
    For the same product, markup % is always the bigger number.
    Pricing off a target
    P = VC ÷ (1 − target margin)
    P = VC × (1 + target markup)
    Fixed vs. variable costs
    Fixed costs don't move with quantity produced (plant, engineers, advertising, salaried staff). Variable costs rise with each unit (materials, production-line labor, packaging, per-unit shipping).
